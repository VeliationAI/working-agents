# Where to put the free edition

Publish the PDF first. Add audio when the voice is one you are allowed to share. LinkedIn is where people discover it. A durable link is where the file actually lives, because a social post scrolls away.

## This week

**LinkedIn, personal profile.** Document posts, a Featured item, and a newsletter. The guide is `publishing/linkedin/README.md`. A company page can reshare you. The original post should be you.

**This OneDrive folder.** Local working copy. Edit the manuscript here, rebuild, then push. Keep OneDrive as your draft desk, not as the durable download link.

## This month

**GitHub.** The public repository is the durable home. Tag `v0.9` for the public draft and `v1.0` when the preface is yours. Each release should attach `The-Agent-Loop.pdf` and `The-One-Page-Agent-Brief.pdf`. Put the release URL in the first comment of the LinkedIn launch post and in Featured.

**Internet Archive.** Upload the PDF, with the author, the title, the license, and the year. It is a free, durable copy. Use it as the link you are willing to put in a slide months from now.

**A single webpage, only if you want one.** A page with the cover, the one-paragraph promise, a download, and the credit line is enough. Do not wait on a website before the first LinkedIn post.

## Audio, after the voice question is settled

**YouTube.** One video per chapter, your voice, the chapter title in the name, captions on, and the PDF linked in the description. A playlist titled Working Agents is the audio edition people can find.

**A podcast feed.** Spotify for Podcasters, or any host that gives you an RSS feed, once you have more than one episode you stand behind. Chapter files are the episodes. Show notes carry the brief and the license.

The private preview in `dist/audio/` is not this step. Read `publishing/audio.md`.

## What to put on every copy

- Title, your name, "Working Agents"
- Edition number and date
- CC BY 4.0 and the license URL
- A contact link for corrections

The PDF builder writes those onto the copyright page from `series.yaml`. If you upload the file to Archive or GitHub, copy the same facts into the description field.

## Print

You do not need an ISBN to give away a PDF. If you later want a paid paperback, that is a separate edition: new ISBN, the same license or a different one, and a note that the free PDF still exists. Keep the free edition free. The visibility comes from the free copy being the one people forward.
