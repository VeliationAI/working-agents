Most things called an AI agent this month are one of three systems.

A chatbot answers, then stops.
A workflow follows steps you wrote down in advance.
An agent keeps going until a finish line, and the next step depends on what it just found.

The test I use in meetings:

If you can write the steps before you see the case, build a workflow. Workflows are easier to test and usually cheaper to run.

If the next step depends on the case, you are looking at an agent. Then you owe the work four things, or you are hoping.

A goal someone can check.
A way to see the facts.
Tools no wider than the job.
A stop, including permission to come back and ask.

"Summarize this PDF" is a workflow. The material is already in your hands.

"Figure out why this customer is unhappy and draft a reply from our policy and their last 90 days of tickets" is agent work. It should draft, and wait, before anything is sent.
