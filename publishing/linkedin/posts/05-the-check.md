The loop is only as honest as its check.

A clear paragraph can pass a busy person and fail every test that mattered. Fluency is a property of the sentences. It is not a property of the facts.

I want the handback in five parts, in this order.

1. The result.
2. The checks that passed.
3. The unknowns.
4. The sources, specific enough to reopen.
5. What it did not do. Especially the message it did not send.

If I have to redo the research to believe the brief, the system saved me no work. It assigned me reading.

"Review your work and fix any issues" is a weak check. It often produces a confident rewrite with new claims. A checklist tied to the original tests is boring. Boring checks get used on Fridays.

Yes or no, with a pointer to the line.

Are the four sections present?
Does every number appear in a source from this run?
Are gaps marked unknown?
Was anything sent?

Approve against that list, or do not approve.
