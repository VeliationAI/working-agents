# The goal is a mood, until you write the finish line

Working Agents, issue 1. The short version of a chapter from The Agent Loop, which is free.

---

You can feel the difference in the handback.

Ask an agent to "help me prepare for the Acme meeting" and you will get something fluent. You will walk into Thursday unable to say which sentences came from the customer record and which ones came from the shape of a briefing the model has seen a thousand times.

Ask for this instead.

A briefing of at most two pages, as a draft, not an email. Four sections: what they bought, open support issues from the last 90 days, unpaid invoices, and three questions I should ask. Sources are the customer record, the support inbox, and billing. If a fact is missing, write unknown. Do not contact the customer. Stop after those sources have been checked.

Now you can be disappointed in a useful way. A missing invoice is a gap. An invented invoice is a failed test. Those are different problems, and only the second version tells them apart.

Four parts, whenever you delegate a job like this:

- Output. The thing that must exist at the end.
- Tests. How you will know it is acceptable.
- Boundaries. What it must not touch.
- Stop. When to quit, including permission to be incomplete.

I would rather carry an incomplete brief into the meeting than a complete-sounding one.

The one-page version of this, blank and filled in, is the handout. The full argument, including tools, memory, and the check, is Book 1, The Agent Loop. Both are free.
