# LinkedIn

LinkedIn will not host a bookstore. It will distribute a person who teaches, on a schedule, with a free file behind the teaching. The book gives you ten weeks of material that is already consistent. The posts give you the reach. Featured and the newsletter give you a shelf that is still there in March.

Use your personal profile. Let a company page reshare you if you have one.

## Before the first post

1. Name and LinkedIn URL in `series.yaml`. Rebuild the PDF so the copyright page matches.
2. Preface note replaced. If you would not defend a sentence in the comments, it is not in the book yet.
3. Featured section: upload `dist/pdf/The-Agent-Loop.pdf`. Title it "The Agent Loop, free field guide." Description: one sentence from the subtitle, plus "PDF, CC BY 4.0."
4. Create a newsletter called **Working Agents**. Description: "Short, free field guides on AI agents that do real work. One idea a week."
5. Headline, pick one and then leave it alone:

- I write Working Agents, free field guides on AI agents that finish real work
- Field guides for people who have to make AI agents actually finish the job
- Working Agents: free books on goals, tools, and the loop behind an AI agent

About section, three short paragraphs: who you help, the loop in one sentence, and where the free PDF is. End with the Featured file rather than a vague "link in bio."

## How to post

Write the idea in the post. Put the download link in the first comment, and again in Featured. A post that is only a link teaches nobody, and a post that teaches still works if the link is quiet.

Reply to comments in the first hour. Answer with the distinction, not with "thanks for sharing."

Weekday morning, in the timezone where your readers work, is enough of a schedule. Two teaching posts and one document or newsletter issue each week will build the series without turning your profile into a feed of announcements.

## What to do with comments that pull you into hype

People will ask which tool is best. Answer with the brief. Ask what done looks like, what the system may touch, and who checks it. You can talk about tools after that, including your own, without pretending the tool was the hard part.

## A note on the draft

Say it is edition 0.9. Readers trust a person who says the text is a public draft and shows the page anyway. They bounce off a person who announces a series that has no file behind it. You have the file.
