This is a short book about a distinction that will save you time.

A chatbot answers, then stops. An agent keeps going until a finish line, or until it is stuck and needs you. The difference sounds small the first time you hear it. It decides what you can delegate, what you must specify, and what can go wrong while you are in another meeting.

Working Agents is a free series of field guides. Each book is meant to be read in an evening and heard on a commute. The PDF and the audio carry the same words, so you can switch between them. This is Book 1, The Agent Loop. Later books go deeper on briefing, tools, memory, real jobs, failure, teams of agents, and shipping. You can use this book without them.

It is written for people who use these systems at work, people who buy or supervise them, and people who build them. You do not need to write code to follow the argument. If you do write code, you still need the argument. A vague goal breaks a careful implementation.

Read it straight through and it takes about an hour. If you are deciding whether a project should be an agent at all, read the chapters on the three kinds of systems, on goals, on the three kinds of work, and on the one-page brief. If you are about to let a system send, edit, or delete something, read the chapters on tools, on the check, and on failures first.
