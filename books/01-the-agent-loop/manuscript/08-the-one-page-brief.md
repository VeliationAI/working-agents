---

This is the page to reuse. Fill it in before you open a tool. If you cannot fill a line, you are not ready to run the loop, and finding that out on paper is cheaper than finding it out in a sent folder.

The page is short on purpose. A brief that tries to contain the whole company will be skipped, by people and by the loop. Put the job in front. Put standing company rules on the standing-notes shelf from the memory chapter, and point to them in one line if you must.

```
JOB
What finished work looks like, in one or two sentences.

OUTPUT
The artifact. Length, format, and where it should land.
A draft, a file, or a ticket. Name the destination.

TESTS
- Sections or fields that must be present
- Format rules, such as dates written YYYY-MM-DD
- Every factual claim has a source, or is marked unknown

BOUNDARIES
Do not: ...
Allowed tools: ...
Write actions that need approval before they run: ...

STOP
Stop when the tests pass.
Stop when a required fact cannot be retrieved.
Stop after this many steps: __
Do not guess in order to fill a gap.

HANDBACK
Return the result, the checks, the unknowns, and the sources.
```

## A filled example

```
JOB
Prepare me for a 30-minute renewal call with Acme on Thursday.

OUTPUT
A briefing of at most two pages, as a document draft. Not an email.

TESTS
- Sections: what they bought, open support issues from the last
  90 days, unpaid invoices, three questions for me to ask.
- Dates written as YYYY-MM-DD.
- Every fact has a source line, or the value unknown.
- No competitor is named.

BOUNDARIES
Do not contact Acme or anyone outside the company.
Allowed tools: customer records (read), support inbox (read),
billing (read).
No write actions.

STOP
Stop after those three sources have been checked, or after 12 steps.
Incomplete is acceptable. Invented is not.

HANDBACK
Brief first, then unknowns, then sources.
```

You can hand that page to a person or to an agent. Read it once as the new teammate. Every question you would still ask belongs in the brief before you run anything.

## What the page is doing

The job and the output keep the loop from inventing a bigger assignment. "Not an email" is doing as much work as the two-page limit.

The tests become the checker. They are boring, and they should be. Boring tests get applied. Clever tests get admired and skipped.

The boundaries are the tool list in ordinary language. If a tool is not on the page, it is not part of this run. Adding a tool means editing the page, which is a moment to think.

The stop protects you from a loop that is almost finished forever. Twelve steps is an example, not a law. Set it high enough for the sources you named, and low enough that a stuck run dies while you are still willing to read the trace.

The handback is the contract for your attention. Result, checks, unknowns, sources. When a run returns a different shape, send it back. You are training the loop, and you are training yourself not to accept fluency as a substitute.

A blank copy of this page is the handout that goes with the book. Print it. Pin it. Make a team fill one in before they ask for another agent demo.
