title: Goals you can finish
---

Agents go quiet in a particular way when the goal is a mood. They return something plausible, and you cannot say whether they finished.

Make this better. Be more strategic. Research the market. Those are fine things to say to a colleague who shares your context. They are unfinished goals for an agent. The loop cannot check them, so it will stop when the prose sounds complete.

## Four parts of a finishable goal

You can write them in one short paragraph.

1. Output. The thing that must exist at the end. A file, a decision memo, a list, a draft waiting for approval.
2. Tests. How a person, or the loop, will know the output is acceptable. Sections, format, sources, constraints.
3. Boundaries. What the loop must leave untouched. Do not email the customer. Do not change the live system. Do not invent a number.
4. Stop. When to quit trying. After the named sources have been checked. When a required fact is missing. After a set number of steps.

A finishable goal can still be the wrong goal. The loop will pursue a bad finish line with the same energy it gives a good one. Spend the time on the finish line.

## The same meeting, two ways

Weak: Help me prepare for the Acme meeting.

Finishable: Produce a briefing of at most two pages for a 30-minute renewal call with Acme on Thursday. Include what they bought, open support issues from the last 90 days, any unpaid invoice, and three questions I should ask. Use only our customer records, the support inbox, and billing. If a fact is missing, write "unknown". Do not contact Acme. Stop after those three sources have been checked, even if the picture is incomplete.

The second version gives the loop something it can observe, a closed set of actions, tests a person can apply, and permission to be incomplete. Incomplete is a usable briefing. Invented is a risk you carry into the room.

Notice the size. Two pages, four sections, three sources. A tight goal is easier to check, and a checked briefing is worth more than a long one nobody can audit before Thursday.

## Asking for range

You will be tempted to leave a goal vague so the system can surprise you. Surprise is a weak default. When you want range, ask for it in a form that can still be finished.

Give me three angles for the renewal call. For each angle, give one reason and one risk. Use only the sources above. If the sources do not support an angle, do not include it.

That request has room in it. It also has a stop. An angle with no source does not get a seat at the table.

Write the goal as if a careful new teammate will follow it on a morning when you are offline. If they would still have to invent your standards, the goal is not finished, and the loop will invent them for you.
