This is a short book about the part of an agent that people most want to be magic and least want to maintain.

Memory sounds like a feature. In practice it is a pile of choices. What is in front of the model right now. What it can look up if it asks. What was written down last month by someone who has since changed teams. What a customer told you in confidence, and when you promised to forget it.

Get those choices wrong and the failure is quiet. The agent does not crash. It offers a discount you stopped offering in the spring. It cites a policy that was replaced. It answers a new customer with the first customer's address. Every sentence is fluent. None of it is current.

Working Agents is a free series of field guides. Each book is meant to be read in an evening and heard on a commute. The PDF and the audio carry the same words. This is Book 4, Memory. Book 1 introduced five shelves in a single chapter: the brief, the trace, the case file, the standing notes, and the archive. This book takes them one job at a time. You can read it without Books 2 and 3. If you have not read Book 1, the first chapter here repeats enough to start.

Here is what you will do with it.

- Sort what you hand an agent onto the five shelves, and find the items that have started to rot.
- Understand retrieval well enough to ask a useful question about it.
- Decide, before the run, which instruction wins when a standing note fights today's brief.
- Put a clock and an owner on customer data, and know what deletion has to reach.
- Notice when a long thread has become a liability, and hand it to a fresh one.
- Write a one-page memory policy a team can follow on a busy Tuesday.

If you have twenty minutes, read the chapter on contradiction and the one-page policy. If you handle other people's data, read the chapter on customer data before you let anything remember anything.

This book is not legal advice. Where it touches retention and deletion, it describes habits that make the legal conversation easier. Your obligations depend on where you operate and whose data you hold. Ask the person who owns that question.

The test of the book is a piece of work you can point at. By the end you should be able to open an agent's memory, read it as a stranger would, and say which lines are true, which are old, and which should not be there at all.
