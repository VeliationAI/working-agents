This book is about one page of writing.

In Book 1, The Agent Loop, the claim was that an agent is a model in a loop: a goal, a way to observe, a way to act, and a check, inside limits you set. That book ended with a one-page brief. This book is what happens when you try to use that page on jobs that are not the example.

You will find out quickly that the page is easy to fill in badly. "Research our competitors" fits in the JOB box. It does not give anyone, human or model, a finish line. The page only works when the lines are specific enough to fail.

Working Agents is a free series of field guides. Each book is meant to be read in an evening and heard on a commute. The PDF and the audio carry the same words. You can read this book without Book 1, but you will need its vocabulary: goal, tool, check, stop, handback. They are defined again in the terms at the back.

## Who this is for

You delegate work to a system that acts. You may be a manager who has never written a prompt. You may be an analyst who writes them daily. You may be an engineer building the harness that runs the loop. The craft in this book is the same for all three, because the brief is the place where the job is agreed.

If you only have twenty minutes, read the chapter on acceptance tests and the three filled briefs. If you run a job every week, read the chapter on the weekly brief. If your team has a prompt in one tool, a checklist in another, and a policy document in a third, read the chapter on one instruction set.

## What you will have at the end

- A brief for a real job from your own week, not an example.
- A list of the facts you forgot to write down, because you have worked there for years.
- Tests you will actually apply, written so that a stranger could apply them.
- One instruction set that serves as brief, prompt, standing note, and checklist without drifting into four documents.

## How to read it

Keep a real task beside you. Each chapter ends in something you can do to that task. When a chapter shows a filled brief, do not copy the wording. Copy the discipline: a named output, tests that can fail, a boundary written as a list, a stop, and a handback that says what is unknown.
