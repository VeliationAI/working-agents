# Series bible

Working Agents is a sequence of short, free field guides. Each book stands alone. Each one should be readable in an evening and listenable on a commute. The PDF and the audio use the same words, so a reader can switch without getting lost.

## Who it is for

People who already use chatbots at work and are now being asked to "use agents," buy them, or build them. Some write code. Many do not. The books have to be true for both, which means the argument comes before the framework.

## The promise

One idea, one page you can use the next morning, and enough judgment to look at a demo and say what it actually is.

## What the series refuses

- Vendor scorecards and feature news. Those expire.
- Framework tutorials as the spine of a book. Name a mechanism when it helps, then return to the job.
- Hype. An agent is a model in a loop. Say that, then talk about the goal, the tools, the check, and the stop.
- Books long enough that nobody finishes the audio.

## Length and shape

About 7,000 to 12,000 words. A preface a person can replace with their own voice. Worked examples from ordinary jobs: a briefing, a support draft, a file rename, a change that a person still approves. A glossary. A credit line. A handout that can travel without the book.

Edition 0.9 means a public draft. Call it 1.0 after you have read it aloud, replaced the preface note, and removed every sentence you do not believe.

## Voice

Second person. Short paragraphs. Define a term, then use it. Prefer a trace, a brief, or a before-and-after goal over an abstraction. When a workflow is the better design, say so. The series loses trust the moment it treats "agent" as a compliment.

## The shelf

1. **The Agent Loop.** What an agent is, and how to tell it from a chatbot or a workflow. Drafted.
2. **Briefing an Agent.** How a real job becomes a brief a person and a loop can share.
3. **Tools and Permissions.** Read, draft, write. Least privilege, secrets, and logs.
4. **Memory.** What stays in view, what is retrieved, and what is deleted.
5. **Agents at Work.** Patterns for research, writing, operations, support, and code.
6. **When the Loop Goes Wrong.** Traces, a small exam from real jobs, and the write-up after a bad run.
7. **More Than One Agent.** Handoffs, and the many times one agent is the better design.
8. **Shipping an Agent.** Cost, fallbacks, and a loop in front of people who did not build it.

Outlines for 2 through 8 are in `books/UPCOMING.md`. Write the next book only after Book 1 is in your own voice and the first posts are live. The audience will tell you which shelf they need next. The outline keeps you from starting over.

## How a book becomes a LinkedIn presence

One chapter a week is enough.

- A text post teaches the idea in under 250 words. The first two lines must make sense before "see more."
- A document post carries an artifact: the brief, a checklist, a trace.
- A newsletter issue goes one level deeper and links the free PDF.
- A short clip, later, is you reading one page. Your voice. The synthetic preview stays in the editing folder.

Book 1 is about ten weeks of teaching if you go slowly, or two weeks if you launch and then settle into a weekly rhythm. Ship the teaching. Keep drafting Book 2 in the same folder.

## Renaming

You can rename the series in `series.yaml` before anyone has shared it. After the first public post, the name is part of the credit line. Pick it on purpose. "Working Agents" is the working title because it says who the books are for: people who need the systems to finish a job.
